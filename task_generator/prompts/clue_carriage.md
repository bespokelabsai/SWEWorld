Below is a remark somebody was supposed to make, and the conversation that was
written to convey it. The remark is deliberately **not** quoted whole in any one
message — it is meant to be spread across the exchange.

Break the remark into its distinct claims, and for each say whether a reader of the
conversation **alone** could recover it.

## What counts as a claim

Only things a reader would have to know in order to build the thing: a name, a
value, a key and its order, a boundary, a file, a shape, a rule about what happens
when. If two people would write different code depending on whether they knew it, it
is a claim.

## What does not count, and must not be listed

The remark is one person's sentence; the conversation is several people talking. So
everything about *how* the sentence was delivered is expected to differ, and none of
it is a claim:

- **who says it.** The remark has one speaker; the exchange splits it deliberately.
  "One person holds both halves", "the speaker owns the checker and proposes the
  fix", "the reviewer is the one who already knows" — not claims.
- **tense and completion.** The remark may report the work as already done. The
  conversation must *not* — the implementation has not happened yet, and a
  conversation that says it has contradicts the ticket. "The pinning is already
  done", "the seeding and the run are one completed act" — not claims, and their
  absence is correct.
- **stance, tone, hedging, and how confident anybody sounds.**
- **discourse glue.** That one part follows from another, that a "so" links two
  halves, that a detail is offered as a consequence rather than a fact. If both
  facts are in the exchange, a reader has what they need.

Listing any of these as missing fails a conversation for doing exactly what it was
asked to do. If the information is all there and only the packaging changed, every
part is present.

Be strict about the real thing, though. A reader who would have to already know the
answer has not recovered it, and a claim gestured at without its specifics — a value
alluded to but never written, a name described but never typed — is absent.
