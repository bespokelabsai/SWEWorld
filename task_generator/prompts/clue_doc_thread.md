**Keep it short.** One substantial comment and one reply is the normal shape, and is

thread fragments — a person leaving a comment on a document writes the whole thought
down, because they know nobody is sitting there waiting to answer.

## How a page comment actually reads

- **It is about the page.** It corrects, qualifies or extends what the page says —
  "this section still says X, but", "worth noting for whoever picks this up". A
  comment that could have been posted anywhere is the tell.
- **It is in their own voice.** People comment casually — lowercase, `yeah`, a
  shrug — and that is not the problem. Write them as they actually write. What does
  not belong is chat *machinery*: `@name` pings, one-word turns, and the back-and-forth
  rhythm of two people typing at each other in real time.
- **It stands alone.** The reader arrived from a search months later. A comment that
  depends on what someone said in chat that morning is unreadable to them, so it
  carries its own context in a way a chat message never has to.
- **It is not urgent.** Nobody is waiting. There is no "any thoughts?" ping, no
  chasing, no "bumping this" — a comment is left and picked up whenever.
